# Goal-conditioned RL under real-world conditions: problem statement

## Problem
(Rewritten 2026-11-20 after the user's correction; the user confirmed the reading it rests on, the paragraph itself is not confirmed yet.)
The user needs a precise definition of the goal-conditioned control problem under the conditions real systems impose, so that their own experiments have a fixed setup and any number of existing or new algorithms can be run and compared under it. The conditions: a goal is a point the agent is asked to go to, and nothing ever says whether it got there. The system gives no reward and no "close enough" signal; the agent is never tested on equality with the goal, by the benchmark or by itself, because that has probability 0; the agent does not report "reached" or a distance; whatever reward it uses internally it has to construct itself. A goal is a full observation; goals in a part of the observation or in a latent space are out of scope. The agent cannot request a reset; the environment can reset it at any time, outside the agent's control, to a starting state sampled from a distribution, and the reset is announced; training is one stream of interaction in which no goals are given; evaluation on goals is done in separate environments and can be run at any time during training, not as a phase after it. Only the absence of any reward or success signal is essential; the other conditions are important but may be relaxed. Methods are graded from outside by a measurement of their behaviour only; what they learned (critic, reward model, world model) is used for diagnostics, not grading. Out of scope: any choice of algorithm, and a specific real system.

The user's words (2026-11-20): "I'd like to formalize the problem of GCRL in the real world: limited rollouts, no reward signal."

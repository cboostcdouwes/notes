# Draft: the owned rack wholly on the rear fork (leader O31, round 10)

1. Remove the old DIY rack.
2. Lower legs of the owned (sound) rack on the mudguard lugs above the dropouts: steel bolts of the lug's thread
   (check size), full engagement, blue threadlock, washer; rack leg against the frame, mudguard rod outside it.
3. Upper struts to the rear fork's tubes, not the seat (it flexes): measure the tube diameter; rubber-lined tube
   clamps of that size (Tubus seat-stay mounts 14-25 mm, or lined P-clamps), or a small bracket; struts bent or
   lengthened to meet them at a steep enough angle to brace, one clamp per side, as far from the lugs as the
   geometry allows. Lugs + clamps on the same rigid fork form a triangle per side.
4. A slack steel cable from each lower leg to the axle, so a stripped lug can't drop the rack onto the tyre.
5. Fit check with both bags (the real use): push the rack top sideways: no sway; cobbles: no rattle; tyre gap > 1 cm;
   rack top clear of the seat at the seat's flex; panniers clear chain and brake; tight corners at walking pace.
   Recheck clamps and bolts after 50 km, then every few months; a loose lug thread → lower legs to the axle (O4c).

Stress-test findings (paper): nothing touches the flexing seat; one rigid structure carries the rack. Weak spots:
clamp sizing on thin 7005 tubes, strut angle. Not tested on the real bike.

# Recumbent luggage carrying

## Problem
The user rides a recumbent bicycle and wants to carry luggage on it. The user's request named the solution ("attach a
luggage rack") and asked how best to attach it; the need behind it is that luggage travels with the bike and rider,
held securely, without making the bike unsafe or unpleasant to ride. Round 2: the bike is an Optima Baron (a
lowracer, see *Facts*), the luggage is commute and shopping, under 15 kg, and the user already owns a rack of a type
not yet known. Round 3: the user's Baron has no rear suspension, and a generic upright rack is already DIY-mounted on
the mudguard bolts and has held so far; the user accepts a mount the maker advises against if it holds. Why the user
wants a better attachment than the one that holds is not known yet. Round 4: the current rack "sways, rattles or
sits badly"; it is "badly made and badly mounted" and the user fears "it's going to fall apart soon". Its top is fixed
to a frame tube. The need is now a sound, stiff rack mounting on this bike, with the current rack or another. Round 5: the user
replaces the rack, buys one and fits it; of the maker's rack points, the user sees only one or is unsure. Round 6:
the user is fine with the mudguard lugs for a new rack's lower legs; the daily load is two bags (coat, lock, tools;
laptop, charger, phone, notebook); the bike is ridden on bike lanes and through tight corners.

Harms of the leader (O23a / O25 on the F4 points), a few steps ahead:
- The seat-frame fixtures carry the rack and the rider; the shell bolts next to them have a 6 Nm limit (F5): don't
  confuse the two when chasing a rattle. If the rubber spacers sit in the rack's joint, the rack can sway on them.
- Changing the seat angle (~5°, F5) moves the rack: re-check clearance and level after each change.
- A rack fixed to the seat fixtures comes off with the seat: note the order of parts when removing the seat.
- O23a may never turn up: put a time limit on the search (e.g. a month), then O25 or O23b.

Harms of O29 (round 6 leader), a few steps ahead:
- A stripped or sheared lug bolt drops the rack onto the tyre (R2): steel bolts, threadlock, a slack backup tether,
  checks every few months; move to O4 at the first loose thread.
- If the lugs sit on a bolted, pivoted rear part rather than a one-piece triangle (unverified), rack loads go into
  that pivot (R5).
- An upper strut on seat-frame fixtures with rubber spacers may bring the old sway back (R10).

Harms of the leader O4 (round 5), a few steps ahead:
- The QR fit kit replaces the rear skewer: a wrongly cut or under-tightened skewer loosens the wheel and the rack
  together. Cut to length per the kit, torque, check after the first ride.
- A rack at the axle on a 26" wheel under a very low seat may sit high relative to the seat, or need the upper strut
  at a steep angle: check the fit before cutting struts; the seat fixture used must hold the strut without loading
  the shell bolts (6 Nm).

# Draft: fitting the user's rack to an Optima Baron (leader O1)

Throwaway draft of the leading option as the user would carry it out, to stress-test it on paper.

1. Mount points: the upper seat fixtures and the bolts half way down the seat support tube (Baron manual p. 12).
   Both are on the sprung main frame, so the rack and the seat move together and the load rides on the suspension.
   Do NOT use the bolt holes at the top of the swingarm above the dropouts: the manual reserves them for mudguard rods
   ("not to be used for racks"), and a rack fixed to both the swingarm and the seat would be strained on every bump.
2. If the rack is an Optima rack: bolt it to those points with the seat; seat bolts stay at max 6 Nm with the rubber
   spacers; use bolts long enough for the rack's tab, not extra torque.
3. If it is a generic upright rack: its lower legs (meant for dropout eyelets) must instead reach the mid seat-support-
   tube bolts, and its upper struts the upper seat fixtures. Shorten or replace the lower legs with flat steel/alu
   strip of the original's section or the rack maker's strut kit; spacer plates where tabs don't line up.
4. Fit check, the real use (one ride unloaded, one with ~15 kg):
   - push the rear down to full suspension compression: tyre-to-rack and tyre-to-pannier gap stays > ~1 cm;
   - with the seat at its used angle (±5° range moves the rack);
   - panniers clear the rider's arms, the chain and the rear brake; heel clearance is a non-issue on a lowracer;
   - getting on and off is unchanged (the rack sits behind the seat);
   - the bike still steers normally at walking speed with 15 kg behind the seat.
5. Threadlocker, recheck after 50 km; write the max load (15 kg) on the rack (O17).

Stress-test findings (paper, round 2):
- Step 1 is the whole answer to "how to attach": the maker's points, not the eyelets an upright-bike habit points to.
- Step 3 is where it can fail: a generic rack's geometry may not reach the F4 points without home-made struts.
- Why the user might reject it in use: the rack may rattle against the seat, or a generic rack may sit too high.
- Not tested on a real bike.

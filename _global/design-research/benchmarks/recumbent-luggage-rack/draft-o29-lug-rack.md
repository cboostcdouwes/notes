# Draft: quality rack on the Baron's lugs + seat fixture (leader O29, round 6)

1. Remove the old DIY rack. Note how its top was clamped to the frame tube.
2. Use the sound rack the user owns for the lugs (or buy a quality 26" rack), with adjustable upper struts.
3. Lower legs on the mudguard lugs above the dropouts: steel bolts of the lug's thread (check size; likely M5), full
   thread engagement, blue threadlock, washer; rack leg against the frame, mudguard rod outside it.
4. Upper struts to the upper seat fixture (the bolts half way down the seat support tube are too far forward for a
   stable strut), metal-to-metal, M5/M6 stainless,
   threadlock; not to the seat-shell bolts (6 Nm). Two upper struts to one fixture plus two lower legs make a
   triangle per side, which is what stops the sway the old rack had.
5. A slack steel cable from each lower leg to the axle or a frame tube, so a stripped lug can't drop the rack onto
   the tyre.
6. Fit check with both bags (the real use): push the rack top sideways: no sway; ride cobbles: no rattle; tyre gap
   > 1 cm; panniers don't widen the bike beyond the bike lane habit, clear chain and brake; tight corners at walking
   pace. Recheck all bolts after 50 km, then every few months; at the first loose lug thread, move to O4 (axle).

Stress-test findings (paper): simplest bought-and-fitted option; the lugs held a badly made rack, a quality rack with
a proper top fixing loads them less in sway. Unverified: lug thread size; whether a 26" rack's struts reach the seat
fixture at a sane angle; where the seat's rubber spacers sit. Not tested on the real bike.

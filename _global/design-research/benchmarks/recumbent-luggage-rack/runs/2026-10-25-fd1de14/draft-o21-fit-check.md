# Draft: keeping the rack on the mudguard lugs, improved (leader O21, round 3)

Throwaway draft of the leading option as the user would carry it out, to stress-test it on paper.

1. Lower legs stay on the mudguard lugs at the top of the rear stays above the dropouts. Use steel bolts of the lug's
   thread (likely M5; check), long enough for full thread engagement but not into the tyre's path; blue threadlocker;
   a washer under the head. If a mudguard rod shares the lug, put the rack leg against the frame and the rod outside.
2. Upper fixing: a rigid bracket to a frame or seat point (the maker's upper seat fixtures, F4, are the natural one),
   not cable ties. Within the 6 Nm seat-bolt limit if it shares the seat bolts.
2b. Backup tether: a short steel cable or strap from each lower rack leg to the axle or a frame tube, slack in normal
   use, so a sheared lug bolt leaves the rack hanging instead of dropping onto the tyre.
3. Fit check with ~15 kg (the real use): rack level; tyre gap > 1 cm when bouncing on the rear; panniers clear chain
   and brake; ride a cobbled stretch, then check every bolt for movement.
4. Recheck after 50 km, then every few months; at the first loose or stripped thread, move the legs to the axle (O4)
   or the maker's points (O1).

Stress-test findings (paper):
- It costs nothing and has a record; its weak point is the thread size of the lugs and the unknown upper fixing.
- Why the user might reject it: it is what they already have, so they presumably asked because something about it
  bothers them (unknown: the deciding question for round 3).
- Not tested on the real bike.

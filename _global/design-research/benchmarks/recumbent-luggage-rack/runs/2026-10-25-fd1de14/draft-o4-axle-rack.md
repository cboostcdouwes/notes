# Draft: bought axle-mounted rack on the Optima Baron (leader O4, round 5)

1. Diagnose the old rack's sway first only if keeping anything from it; otherwise remove it, and leave the mudguard
   lugs to the mudguard rods.
2. Buy an axle-mounted rack with a QR fit kit (Old Man Mountain: adjustable QR skewer, pucks for frames without upper
   eyelets, 33 kg axle-mounted), or a Tubus rack with the Tubus QR adapter (Tubus racks only, 25 kg, 130/135 mm hubs).
   Before buying: measure the rear dropout spacing and QR length, and the height from the axle to the upper seat
   fixture, against the rack's strut range.
3. Lower end: the rack's feet on the fit kit's axle studs, skewer cut and tightened per the kit.
4. Upper end: the strut to the upper seat fixture that is there (the maker's rack point, F4), metal-to-metal, M5/M6
   stainless, threadlock; not to the seat-shell bolts (6 Nm). If that fixture can't take it, the kit's pucks on a
   frame tube.
5. Fit check with ~15 kg: push the rack top sideways (no sway), cobbles (no rattle), tyre gap > 1 cm, chain and brake
   clear; recheck after 50 km.

Stress-test findings (paper): the axle carries the load the lugs weren't meant for; one upper point plus two axle
feet form a triangle per side, which is what stops sway. Unverified: whether the kit and rack fit a 26" wheel under a
very low seat. Not tested on the real bike.

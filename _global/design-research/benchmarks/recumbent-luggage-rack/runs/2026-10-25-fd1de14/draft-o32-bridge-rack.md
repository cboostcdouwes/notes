# Draft: the owned rack wholly on the rear fork, bolted (leader O32, round 12)

1. Remove the old DIY rack (lugs + a clamp on a frame tube).
2. Lower legs of the owned sound rack on the mudguard lugs above the dropouts: steel bolts of the lug's thread,
   full engagement, blue threadlock, washer; rack leg against the frame, mudguard rod outside it.
3. Top stay bolted through the hole in the bridge between the rear fork tubes, above the wheel (the standard
   upright-bike mount). If the rack's own stay doesn't reach, a flat steel stay cut to length; if the hole carries a
   mudguard or brake, a longer bolt with a spacer so both share it.
4. Nothing goes to the seat, the seat fixtures or the seat support tube: the shell flexes and the seat angle gets
   re-adjusted; the rear fork is one rigid part.
5. A slack steel cable from each lower leg to the axle, so a stripped lug can't drop the rack onto the tyre.
6. Fit check with both bags: push the rack top sideways (no sway); cobbles (no rattle); tyre gap > 1 cm; rack top
   clear of the seat back at every seat angle used; panniers clear chain and brake; tight corners at walking pace.
   Recheck bolts after 50 km, then every few months.

Stress-test findings (paper): every joint bolted, all on one rigid part, independent of seat flex and seat angle;
the lugs have years of evidence. Weak spots: lug thread size, bridge hole position. Not tested on the real bike.

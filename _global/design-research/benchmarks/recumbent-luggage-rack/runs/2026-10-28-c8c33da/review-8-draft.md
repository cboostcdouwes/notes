I've updated the decision: **fit your own rack to your Baron**. It's the cheapest option, and you already know it fits the lugs.

**Updated choice:**
- **Your rack** bolts to the threaded holes by the rear axle. Its legs carry the weight.
- **The upper struts** are fastened to the round rear-fork tubes with Tubus seat-stay clamps, which are rubber-lined. They brace the rack instead of loading your seat mounting.
- **Your own panniers** hang on it, and you fit it yourself.
- **Cost:** only the clamps (about €10–24 a pair) and maybe new stainless M5 bolts, so about €10–25 in total.

**Check on your rack before fitting** (now in `fitting-plan.md`):
- **Load rating:** it's often stamped on the platform, sometimes with EN 14873 or ISO 11243. It should be well above your 15 kg. If there's no marking, the loaded test ride in `fit-check.md` is your check.
- **Pannier hooks:** they should sit firmly on the rack's rails.
- **Rear brake:** the rack's legs must clear it.
- **Struts:** they must reach the rear-fork tubes at a steep angle, not a long flat one.

**Clamp size:** measure the tube diameter where the struts will meet it, then buy that size. The sizes are 14, 16, 18–19, 21–22 and 24–25 mm.

**If the struts don't reach well:** longer struts, or bending the ones you have if the rack's maker allows it, cost only a few euros. The last fallback is the Optima Lowracer bag (€39.95), which means giving up your own panniers.

**Known gaps:** I don't know your rack's rating or how far its struts reach, and no source covers how the bike handles loaded. The loaded test ride covers all three.

**What I missed:** I should have asked in the first round whether you already had a rack or other parts. That would have saved the rack shopping.

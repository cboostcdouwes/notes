# Fitting a rear rack on the Optima Baron (draft)

Upper anchor: Tubus seat-stay clamps on the rear stays (not the seat-bracket holes, which hold the seat).

Rack: your own rack, which fits the dropout lugs. Look for a load rating on it (often stamped on the platform, with
EN 14873 / ISO 11243); it should be well above your 15 kg. Check its rails take your pannier hooks and its legs clear
the rear brake. Clamps: Tubus seat-stay clamps in the measured size (~€10–24 a pair). Struts: your rack's own are too
short, so buy two longer ones, bolted to the holes/tabs at the front of the rack top. Measure from those holes to
the highest point on the rear-fork tubes where a clamp fits: up to 350 mm, Tubus aluminium struts (8 mm, M5 eye,
240 or 350 mm, ~€3–4 each); longer, two strips of flat aluminium or steel bar (about 20 × 3 mm, a few euros at a
hardware store) drilled for M5 at both ends.

## Before buying (10 minutes, tape measure)
1. Sit on the bike in your riding position (the seat flexes when you lean back).
2. Measure, straight up from the rear dropout hole, the height to the underside of the seat back where it is above
   the wheel. The rack top sits about 37 cm above the dropout on a 26"–28" wheel (Tubus figure; check the chosen
   rack's sheet). You need that height **plus 1–2 cm**.
   - Enough room: go on.
   - Not enough: the rack top would hit the seat. Stop; ask a recumbent dealer or frame builder for a custom
     bolt-on rack for your holes (O16).
3. Find the upper anchor: the stays running from the rear dropouts up towards the frame. Measure their diameter at
   the height of the rack top (Tubus seat-stay clamps come in 14, 16, 18–19, 21–22 and 24–25 mm). The struts must
   reach the stays at a steep angle (not long and flat). No stays there: stop; custom rack (O16).
4. Note the bolt size of the dropout holes (most likely M5) and the rear brake type.

## Fitting (hex keys, thread-locker)
1. Bolt the rack's legs to the dropout holes with stainless bolts and medium-strength thread-locker; use spacers if
   the legs touch the derailleur or brake.
2. Set the leg length so the rack top is level and clears the tyre by at least 2–3 cm (more with a mudguard).
3. Fit the Tubus clamps (with their rubber liner, or a strip of inner tube) to the stays and bolt the struts to their
   outer holes. Tighten just enough not to slip; overtightening dents thin aluminium. Bend or cut the struts only as
   the rack's manual allows. Leave the seat-bracket bolts alone: they hold the seat.
4. Push down on the rack and wiggle it sideways: no flex at the bolts, nothing touching the tyre or seat.

## Loaded test (before the first real ride)
Run steps 3–6 of `fit-check.md`: panniers clear spokes and derailleur, getting on and off and pedalling unhindered,
a tight turn at walking pace, a short fast stretch, firm braking. Re-tighten all bolts after the first 50 km.

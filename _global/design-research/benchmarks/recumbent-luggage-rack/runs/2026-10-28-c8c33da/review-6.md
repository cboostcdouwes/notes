## Draft decision: luggage on your Optima Baron

**Choice:**
- **Rack:** a universal rear rack, the **Topeak Uni Super Tourist DX 2.0**. It's rated for 26 kg and its legs adjust in length. Pick the disc or non-disc version to match your rear brake.
- **Legs:** they bolt to the threaded holes by the rear axle and carry the weight.
- **Struts:** they're fastened to the round rear-fork tubes with **Tubus seat-stay clamps**. These are rubber-lined, come in sizes from 14 to 25 mm, and you choose the size to fit the tube.
- **Bags and fitting:** your own hook panniers hang on it, and you fit it yourself.

**Why**, each point resting on something you confirmed:
- **No permanent changes:** bolts go into existing holes and the clamps come off again. Nothing is drilled or welded.
- **Nothing loads the seat mounting:** the struts brace the rack from the rear-fork tubes right next to it, not from the seat bracket.
- **Your load fits:** under 15 kg on a rack rated for 26 kg.
- **Heels and getting on and off stay clear:** the bags sit behind the seat, and your feet are at the front cranks.
- **Your own panniers, fitted with hex keys.**

**Rejected:**
- **Struts to the seat-bracket holes:** too far forward, and they belong to the seat mounting.
- **Optima's own rack:** none exists for the Baron. Your manual confirms that.
- **Rack on the seat, welded-on mounts:** permanent changes.
- **Clamps as the rack's only mounts:** the clamps would carry the whole weight.
- **Struts clamped to the main tube:** long, flat struts, the same weakness as the seat bracket.
- **Optima Lowracer bag:** it would replace your panniers.
- **Underseat and front racks:** no product exists for the Baron, so it would be custom work.
- **Also out:** small bags (too small), a backpack (pressed against the seat), a trailer.

**Fallback:** a custom rack for the axle holes from a recumbent dealer or frame builder, which you bolt on yourself. You'd need it if any of these turn up when you measure:
- the rear-fork tubes are outside 14–25 mm;
- the struts meet them at a flat angle;
- the rack's top doesn't clear the seat.

**Known gaps:**
- **Handling with the load:** no source covers it. Your loaded test ride decides it.
- **Still unmeasured:** the tube diameter and the strut angle.
- **No load limit for the Tubus clamps:** no source states one. They only brace the rack; the legs carry the weight.
- **Assumed:**
  - that the fallback's cost and waiting time would be acceptable (you didn't know your budget);
  - that quick removal and keeping things dry or theft-safe don't matter to you.

**Plan:**
1. **Measure**, following "Before buying" in `fitting-plan.md`: room under the seat for the rack top, the tube diameter and strut angle, the bolt size, and the brake type.
2. **Buy** the rack in the right brake version and the clamps in the measured size.
3. **Fit** it, following "Fitting" in `fitting-plan.md`. Tighten the clamps only until they don't slip; too tight dents aluminium.
4. **Loaded test ride** from `fit-check.md`, and re-tighten everything after 50 km.
5. **After a few weeks of use,** tell me how it went: any clamp slip, how it handles, clearance. Then I'll amend the decision or reopen the research.

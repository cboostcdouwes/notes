**Updated choice:**
- **Rack:** a cheaper rack for 26–28" wheels, rated 25 kg. Your load is still well under that.

- **Mounting stays the same:** the legs bolt to the holes by the rear axle, and the struts are fastened to the round rear-fork tubes with Tubus seat-stay clamps.
- **Clamps:** about €10–24 a pair. The price snapshots I found are from different dates, so that range is uncertain.
- **Total:** about €35–50 with bolts and shipping. Order the rack and clamps from one shop if you can, to pay shipping only once.

**What changed in the decision:**
- **The custom rack is out:** a builder's custom rack can't be made for under €50. That's my estimate; I didn't ask anyone for a quote.
- **New fallback**, in case the tube size, the strut angle or the room under the seat doesn't work out: first a second-hand rack whose struts reach better. If that fails too, the Optima Lowracer bag (€39.95). That gives up your own panniers, but it's the only option left within budget that's made for the Baron.

**Before ordering:**
- **Check the rack's listing:** make sure it states the 25 kg rating and a test standard (EN 14873 or ISO 11243). Each budget rack's rating and price comes from only one shop.
- **Cheap is not the same as weak:** one Baron owner found a cheap adjustable rack weak. A rack rated and tested for 25 kg is a different thing, but the loaded test ride in `fit-check.md` still matters.
